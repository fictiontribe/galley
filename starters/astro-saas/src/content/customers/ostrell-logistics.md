Ostrell Logistics moves chilled and frozen food for supermarkets and restaurant groups from fourteen depots. Every one of those depots depends on refrigeration plant, loading-bay doors and fire systems that have to be checked on time — and, when an auditor visits, shown to have been checked on time.

## Fourteen depots, fourteen ways of working

Before Ferrow, each depot manager kept their own spreadsheet. Some tracked refrigeration checks weekly, some monthly; some recorded readings, some just a tick. "Nobody was doing anything wrong," says Ellie Strand, Ostrell's operations manager. "But I couldn't tell you on a Monday morning which depots were behind, and neither could they."

Audits were the hard part. Pulling together a year of temperature records for one depot meant a week of searching shared drives and inboxes.

## One template, applied everywhere

Ostrell's onboarding started with the asset lists the depots already had. Ferrow's onboarding team merged them into one register of 6,200 assets, and Ellie's team agreed a single set of inspection templates with the depot managers — the same checks, the same intervals, the same readings everywhere.

Within four weeks every depot was inspecting on the Ferrow app. Because the app works offline, checks inside cold stores and plant rooms are recorded where they happen, then sync when the engineer walks back into signal.

## What changed

- **Overdue inspections fell by 58%** over the first year, measured against the year before.
- **Depot managers save around six hours a week** that used to go on chasing and copying paperwork.
- **Audit preparation now takes a morning.** The temperature history for any cold store is exported as one pack, with every reading, photo and signature.

"The best sign," Ellie says, "is that the depot managers stopped asking me what's due. They open Ferrow and it tells them."

## What's next

Ostrell is connecting its refrigeration sensors to Ferrow this winter, so that a reading outside its limits raises a work order on its own — before anyone has to notice.

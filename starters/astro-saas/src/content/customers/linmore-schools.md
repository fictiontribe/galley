Linmore Schools runs 22 primary and secondary schools, from Victorian buildings to a campus finished last year. A nine-person estates team is responsible for every boiler, fire alarm, water outlet and climbing frame across all of them.

## The certificate nobody saw coming

Dana Whitcombe, Linmore's head of estates, remembers the inspection that changed things. "A gas safety certificate for one school kitchen had lapsed by three weeks. The work had been done — the engineer just hadn't sent the paperwork, and nobody had noticed it was missing."

Certificates lived in a shared drive, named however the contractor had named them. Expiry dates lived in someone's calendar.

## Every certificate filed against its asset

Linmore's onboarding put every certificate the team could find into Ferrow's register, attached to the asset it covered. Ferrow read the expiry dates and forecast every renewal for the next eighteen months, so the team could book contractors in quiet weeks instead of rushing in term time.

Contractors now upload their certificates through the Ferrow portal when they finish the job. If one is missing, the job stays open — and Dana's team sees it the same day.

## What changed

- **No lapsed certificates in 2026**, across all 22 schools.
- **Audit preparation fell from two weeks to an afternoon.** Each school's evidence is exported as one pack.
- **96% of statutory checks are done on time**, up from a figure nobody could calculate before.

"The register is the thing I'd miss most," Dana says. "I know what's due in March, and I know who's coming to do it."

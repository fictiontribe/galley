Every estates team has a maintenance plan. Far fewer have a plan they would be happy to hand to an inspector unprepared. The difference is rarely the plan itself — it is whether the plan and the proof of the work live in the same place.

These are the five habits we see in the teams that walk into audits calmly.

## 1. Every task belongs to an asset

"Check fire doors monthly" is an intention. "Check fire door FD-014, second-floor corridor, every month" is a task someone can be held to. When each task is attached to a specific asset, two things become possible: you can see the full history of that asset, and you can prove that nothing on your register was skipped.

If you are starting from scratch, start with the asset register, not the calendar.

## 2. Intervals come from a source

Write down where each interval comes from — a regulation, an insurer's condition, a manufacturer's manual or your own risk assessment. When an inspector asks why the emergency lighting is tested monthly, "because the standard says so, here it is" is a far better answer than "we always have".

## 3. Late is recorded as late

It is tempting to move a missed task to next week and say no more about it. Don't. A schedule that quietly reschedules its misses will always look perfect and will never be believed. Record the original due date, the date it was done and the reason for the gap. Inspectors expect some lateness; what worries them is lateness nobody noticed.

## 4. Evidence is captured at the time

A photo taken during the check, a reading typed in on the spot and a signature from the person who did it are worth far more than a spreadsheet tick added on Friday afternoon. Capture evidence where the work happens, and attach it to the task straight away.

> The best audit trail is one nobody had to assemble.

## 5. Someone reviews the gaps every week

Pick a time each week — Monday morning works for most teams — to look at three lists: what is overdue, what is due in the next fortnight and which certificates expire in the next three months. Fifteen minutes of attention here saves weeks of scrambling later.

## Putting it together

None of this needs special software to begin with. But once your estate passes a few dozen buildings, keeping assets, intervals, evidence and reviews in step by hand stops being realistic. That is the point at which a single schedule, with the proof attached to the work, starts paying for itself.

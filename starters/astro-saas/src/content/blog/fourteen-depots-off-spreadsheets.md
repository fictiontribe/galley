When Ostrell Logistics asked us to bring fourteen cold-chain depots onto Ferrow in four weeks, we said yes, then spent a long evening wondering whether we had been wise. It went well — you can read [their side of the story](/customers/ostrell-logistics/) — but not everything went to plan. Here is what we learned.

## Start with the assets everyone agrees on

Each depot had its own spreadsheet, and each spreadsheet described the same kinds of equipment in different words. We spent the first week agreeing names for twelve asset types before importing a single row. It felt slow. It saved us at least a fortnight later.

## The templates are a conversation, not a document

We arrived with good inspection templates for refrigeration plant. The depot managers improved every one of them. The best change came from a manager who pointed out that our door seal check asked for a photo of the wrong side of the door. Templates written with the people who use them get used.

## What did not go to plan

**Signal.** Two depots had no mobile signal inside the cold stores at all. This was one of the reasons we made offline mode a priority for the autumn release.

**Old habits.** For the first fortnight, three depots kept their spreadsheets "just in case" and filled in both. We should have agreed a switch-off date on day one. We now do, for every customer.

## What we would tell the next team

- Agree the names of things before you import anything.
- Write templates with the people who will use them.
- Set the date the old system stops, and keep it.

Four weeks was the right length. Any shorter and the templates would not have been agreed; any longer and the spreadsheets would have won.

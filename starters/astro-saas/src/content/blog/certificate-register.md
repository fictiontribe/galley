Most lapsed certificates are not missed inspections. The engineer came, the work was done, and the certificate is sitting in someone's inbox or the back of a van. The problem is the register, not the work.

## Know what should exist

A register is only complete if you know what it should contain. For each asset, record which certificates it needs and how often — gas safety yearly, electrical installation every five years, lifting equipment every six months. Now a missing certificate is a visible gap, not an absence nobody notices.

## File by asset, not by contractor

Contractors file paperwork their own way. If your register follows their naming, you will never find anything. File each certificate against the asset it covers, with the date of issue and the date of expiry as separate fields.

## Make the job wait for the paper

A job is not finished until its certificate is filed. If the work order stays open until the document arrives, missing paperwork shows up in your weekly review instead of at the inspection.

## Look three months ahead

Review expiries on a rolling three-month window. That is long enough to book a contractor in a quiet week, and short enough that the list is actionable.

## Keep the history

When a certificate is renewed, keep the old one. Inspectors often ask for the previous period, and an unbroken run of certificates is the strongest evidence you have.

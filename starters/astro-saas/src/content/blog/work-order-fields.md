I spent eight years running a maintenance contractor's service desk. The jobs that dragged on for weeks almost always started the same way: a vague email, missing one or two details that took days to recover.

If your work orders carry these five fields from the first minute, most of that delay disappears.

## 1. The asset, not just the room

"The heating in room 12 isn't working" could be a radiator, a valve, the boiler or a thermostat. Ask the reporter to pick the asset — a QR label on the equipment makes this a ten-second job — and the contractor arrives with the right parts.

## 2. What the person saw

Not a diagnosis, just a description: "water on the floor under the sink, about a cupful, still dripping". A photo is better still. Engineers would rather judge for themselves than act on a guess.

## 3. The priority, from the contract

Priorities chosen in the moment drift towards "urgent". Set priority levels once, tie each to a response time in your contracts, and let the person reporting choose from those definitions.

## 4. Access details

Who has the key, when the room is free, whether the area needs a permit. Missing access details are the single biggest cause of wasted visits we saw.

## 5. Who signs it off

Decide in advance who confirms a job is done — the site manager, the person who reported it or the contractor's own supervisor. A job without an owner for sign-off stays "nearly finished" forever.

## A short checklist

- Asset, chosen from the register
- Description and a photo
- Priority from the contract
- Access details
- Who signs it off

Put these five fields at the top of your work order form, and make the first two required. Your contractors will notice within a month.

    eyebrow: How it started
    heading: Forty is not a marketing number
    layout: two-column
    dropCap: true
    text: |-
      Hesper Lowe spent twelve years cutting coats for other people, most of them in sizes from 6 to 18 and quantities that started at two thousand. The coats were good. Almost nobody who made them ever saw one worn.

      Caddow began with a single coat, the field coat, cut forty times on a borrowed table. Forty is what one cutter can make in a month without hurrying, and what one roll of good Shetland will give if you lay the pattern carefully. It is also, it turned out, about how many people a year want a plain undyed coat badly enough to write to a workroom about it.

      We have kept to forty since. A coat that sells out is made again the next year, usually with one thing changed: a deeper pocket, a lower button, a collar that sits flatter in wind. Nothing is put on sale, because nothing is overmade.

      **Every Caddow piece can come back to us.** Moth, a torn lining, a lost button, a hem that needs letting down — we mend it for the cost of the post, for as long as we are here.

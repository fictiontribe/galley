People assume forty is a marketing number, chosen because small runs sound exclusive. It is not. It is arithmetic.

## One roll, one month

A roll of the Shetland we use for the field coat is about sixty metres. Lay the pattern carefully, nesting the sleeves into the gaps between the fronts, and sixty metres gives you forty-one coats and a long strip of offcuts. We keep the forty-first as the sample. The offcuts become the pocket bags of the next run.

Forty is also what one person can cut, and three people can sew, in a month, without staying late. We tried fifty once. The buttonholes got worse.

## What forty does not mean

It does not mean the coat is gone forever when it sells out. The field coat has been made every year since 2019, forty at a time. When a run sells out, the next one goes on the table; you can put your name down and we will write when it is ready.

It does not mean a sale, either. Because we never make more than we can sell, we never have a rail of coats to get rid of in January. The price is the price all year.

## What it does mean

It means we know most of the people wearing our coats by name. It means that when someone writes to say the pocket is too shallow for a paperback, we can fix it in the next forty. The field coat's pocket is now four centimetres deeper than it was in 2019, because of eleven letters.

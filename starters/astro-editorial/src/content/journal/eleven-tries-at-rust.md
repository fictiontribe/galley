The brief for the car coat was one line long: the colour of a harbour chain after rain. Not orange, not brown, not the red-brown of a flowerpot. The colour iron goes when it has been wet and dry and wet again for forty years.

## The first ten

The first swatch came back from the dyer looking like a pumpkin. The second was better and the third was worse. By the fifth we had stopped asking for "rust" and started posting the dyer things instead: a flake of paint from a bollard, a page torn from a book about shipwrecks, a photograph of the chain itself, taken at four in the afternoon in October.

Swatches six to nine were all close, and all wrong in the same way: too even. Real rust is never one colour. It is a dozen colours that agree with each other from a distance.

## The eleventh

The answer, in the end, was not in the dye at all. Swatch eleven is a grey-brown Shetland, undyed in the fleece, that is then dyed rust in the piece. The natural grey underneath comes through the colour the way old iron comes through new paint. From across the street it is rust. Up close it is rust, and grey, and a little green where the light catches the twill.

It costs more. It takes the mill an extra week. We have pinned all eleven swatches to the board by the workroom door, in order, so we remember why.

## What it means for the coat

Because the colour sits in a grey wool, it will not fade the way a bright dye fades; it will soften, slowly, towards the grey. In ten years your coat will be a slightly different colour from everybody else's. We think that is the point.

In February 2024 the boiler started locking out on cold mornings. The engineer said it had a year or two left and quoted for a new one, which was the easy answer. I wanted to see the others first.

## The four options

I priced the four systems that made sense for a semi with no gas to the cooker, a small garden and a loft that was by then well insulated. For each I took the 2023 heat demand from the readings — about 13,400 kWh of heat — and worked out what each would use to make it.

The storage heaters were out quickly: cheap to keep, but they turn one unit of electricity into one unit of heat, which at 2024 prices was the most expensive way to warm the house by far.

## Fifteen years, all in

Fitting prices only tell you the first day. Over fifteen years, adding fitting, energy and servicing — and a replacement boiler at year twelve where one is needed — the order changes.

The boiler's total includes a second boiler at year twelve, which is why its fitting bar is taller than its quote. Even if gas got cheaper, the heat pump stayed ahead in every version I tried.

> [!WARNING]
> These figures only hold because the loft and draughts were done first. Before that work, the same heat pump would have needed water at 60 °C or more and its running cost would have been close to the boiler's. See [what the meter readings said](/blog/a-year-of-meter-readings/).

## What I chose, and why

```verdict
title: Air-source heat pump, 7 kW
score: 4.5
outOf: 5
summary: The cheapest over its life, the only option that cuts carbon properly, and quieter than I feared. It asked more of the house than a boiler would have.
pros:
  - About £250 a year cheaper to run than a new boiler, at 2024 prices
  - No gas standing charge once the meter is capped
  - Steady, even warmth — the house never has a cold morning now
cons:
  - Three days of work and two new radiators
  - Needs a hot-water cylinder and the cupboard to put it in
  - The installer's first design was oversized; I had to push back
bestFor: Houses that have had their loft and draughts done and have room for a cylinder. If your radiators can't heat the house at 55 °C, fix that first.
```

After the [thermal camera](/blog/what-the-thermal-camera-saw/), I had a list. None of it needed a tradesperson and most of it needed a tube of something and an afternoon. These are the twelve, in the order of how much I think each saved.

## What it saved

I can't measure each fix on its own, but I can estimate from where the cold was and how big the gaps were. The total I can measure: about 11% less gas over the following winter, after allowing for the weather.

> [!WARNING]
> Don't seal the vents in a room with a gas fire, a boiler or an open flue, and keep extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom working. A tight house needs its air changed on purpose.

## What I'd skip

```proscons
title: Worth it, and not
prosLabel: Do these first
consLabel: Wouldn't bother again
pros:
  - Anything on the front door — the biggest change for the least money
  - Caulking skirting boards on a suspended timber floor
  - A chimney balloon, if the fireplace is never used
cons:
  - Radiator foil — I couldn't see any difference in the readings
  - Socket gaskets — fiddly, and the gaps were tiny
  - Self-adhesive foam on the sashes; the brush seals were far better
```

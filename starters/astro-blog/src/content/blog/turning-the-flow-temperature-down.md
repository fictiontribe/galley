A boiler set to 75 °C heats a radiator fast. A heat pump works best at 45–50 °C, and the worry with an old house is that the radiators are too small to heat the rooms at that temperature. You can find out before spending a penny: turn the boiler down and see what happens.

## What I changed

On the first of October I set the boiler's central-heating temperature from 75 °C to 55 °C and left the thermostat where it was, at 20 °C. Nothing else. A cheap thermometer went into each room and I wrote down the readings at 8 a.m. and 8 p.m. through the winter.

## What happened

The house was slower to warm up in the morning — about forty minutes instead of twenty — so I moved the heating's start time earlier. Otherwise it felt the same in most rooms. Gas use fell by about 8% against the same months the year before, adjusted for the weather.

Two rooms weren't the same. The back bedroom, over the kitchen with its cold floor, sat at 17 °C on the three coldest mornings. The front room, with its big single-glazed bay, managed 18.5 °C.

> [!TIP]
> If your boiler has a "weather compensation" setting, use it instead of a fixed temperature. It runs cooler on mild days and warmer on cold ones, which is how a heat pump behaves too.

That week settled it. Most radiators were fine. The two that weren't went on the list for the heat pump installer, and the [heat pump decision](/blog/heat-pump-or-boiler/) came next.

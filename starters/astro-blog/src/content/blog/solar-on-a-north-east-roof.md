The house's only big roof faces north-east. Two of the three installers who came out said it wasn't worth it. The third said "less than you'd hope, more than you'd think," and that turned out to be right.

The shape is the problem with solar on a heat-pump house: it makes the most in the months the house needs least. From May to August the panels made more than the house used, and the spare went to the grid for a few pence a unit.

## Questions I had before

```faq
title: Before you get a quote
items:
  - question: Is a north-east roof worth it at all?
    answer: At current panel prices, usually yes — panels are cheap enough that 70% of a south roof's output still pays back within their life. Ask for the expected yield in kWh, not just the size in kW.
  - question: Do I need a battery?
    answer: I didn't get one. With a heat pump, the evening and overnight use is in winter, when there is little solar to store. A battery made more sense on a time-of-use tariff than for the panels.
  - question: What about shading from the chimney?
    answer: Ask for panels with their own optimisers, or a design that keeps the shaded ones on a separate string. One shaded panel can pull a whole string down.
  - question: How long did fitting take?
    answer: Scaffolding one day, panels and inverter the next, and the scaffold down at the end of the week.
```

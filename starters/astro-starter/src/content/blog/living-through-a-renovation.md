*We asked Ines Ferreira, whose family stayed at home through a nine-month renovation and garden studio build, to share what she wished someone had told her at the start. Over to Ines.*

When the architect warned us that living through the building work would be "challenging", I nodded politely and assumed she was exaggerating. She was not. We survived, and we love the house, but there are things I would do differently.

## Make one room a refuge

Pick one room the builders will not need and make it entirely dust-free: sofa, television, a door that shuts. Ours was the back bedroom. On the worst days — the day the steel went in, the week without a back wall — it was the only thing standing between us and a hotel bill.

## Set up a temporary kitchen early

Do it before the old kitchen comes out, not the morning after. Ours was in the dining room:

- a microwave, a two-ring hob and a kettle on a folding table;
- a washing-up bowl and the garden tap for rinsing;
- a crate of plates, cups and cutlery for each person, and nothing more.

We ate a great deal of soup. It was fine.

## Agree the rules with the builder

Ask on the first day which entrance the team will use, which toilet, what time they start and when the music goes on. It feels awkward to raise, but every builder we spoke to said they would much rather know. A short list on the fridge settled nearly every disagreement before it started.

> Nobody tells you how much of a renovation is just conversation. The building is the easy part.

## Expect one bad week

Something will go wrong. For us it was a delayed delivery of roof glazing that left a tarpaulin over the kitchen for eight days in November. Knowing that a bad week is normal — and that it ends — made it far easier to bear.

## Keep a photo diary

Take a photo from the same spot every day. It is reassuring when progress feels slow, useful when you need to find a pipe hidden behind new plaster, and lovely to look back on once the dust has settled.

## Would we do it again?

Yes, but I would book a week away in the middle.

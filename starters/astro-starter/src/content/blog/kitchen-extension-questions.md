Most people who call us about a kitchen extension have already decided how big it should be. Very few have decided what it is *for*. That is the question that shapes everything else, so we ask it first — along with four more.

## 1. What do you do in the kitchen now?

Not what you would like to do — what you actually do. Who cooks, and how often? Where do the children do their homework? Where does the post pile up? An honest answer to this question tells us more than any mood board.

## 2. What is wrong with the room you have?

Often the problem is not size. It is a door in the wrong place, a window that faces a fence, or a layout that forces everyone through the cooking zone to reach the garden. Some of our happiest clients ended up with a smaller extension than they asked for, because the real problem was solved by moving one wall.

## 3. How much garden can you spare?

Every square metre of extension is a square metre less garden. For a narrow terrace, a side-return extension can add a surprising amount of room while keeping the lawn intact. For a larger plot, a deeper extension might be fine. Either way, it is worth standing at the back door and imagining the new wall before falling in love with a plan.

## 4. Where will the light come from?

A deep extension can make the middle of the house darker than it was before. We almost always add some combination of:

1. a rooflight over the point where old and new meet,
2. a tall window or door that faces the best part of the garden,
3. a lower sill somewhere for sitting and looking out.

The aim is a room that feels bright at the back *and* in the middle.

## 5. What is your real budget?

Not the figure you hope to spend, but the figure you could spend without lying awake at night. Building costs vary enormously with finishes, and the kitchen itself can cost as much as the walls around it. Knowing the real number early lets us design something that fits it, rather than something you have to cut back later.

> The best brief we ever received was half a page long and began, "We are tired of eating dinner in a corridor."

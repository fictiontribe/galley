"Do I need planning permission?" is the question we hear most often, and the honest answer is usually "it depends". Here is what it depends on, explained without the jargon. The rules differ from place to place, so treat this as a starting point rather than advice for your particular house.

## Two routes to the same place

Broadly, there are two ways to get the go-ahead for an extension or loft conversion.

**Full planning permission** means submitting drawings to the local council, who consult the neighbours and decide whether the design is acceptable. It takes around eight weeks and gives the council a say over size, appearance and materials.

**Permitted development** covers smaller changes that are allowed automatically, within strict limits on size, height and position. No application is needed — although we nearly always recommend applying for a certificate confirming the work is lawful, because buyers' solicitors will ask for it.

## When permitted development does not apply

The automatic rights are reduced or removed altogether in several common situations:

- the house is listed, or stands in a conservation area;
- the property is a flat or maisonette rather than a house;
- earlier owners have already used up some of the allowance;
- the council has removed the rights for your street or estate.

If any of these apply, you will almost certainly need a full application.

## The space in between

The interesting part is the grey area. A design that is just over the permitted size might be far better than one squeezed under it — and perfectly likely to be approved. We often draw both, so clients can compare a quick, certain route with a slower one that produces a better room.

> The question is not only "what are we allowed to do?" but "what would actually make the house better?"

## What about the neighbours?

Whichever route you take, it is worth talking to the neighbours early. Many extensions also need a party wall agreement, which is a separate process from planning. A friendly conversation over the fence before any drawings appear has saved more projects than any clever piece of design.

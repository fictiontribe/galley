How to keep a wool coat for 20 years

Brush it, hang it, air it, and almost never wash it. Saoirse Venn on looking after wool, and what to do about moth.

Words by Saoirse Venn/Published30 May 2026/1 min read

A good wool coat should outlast the person who bought it. Most do not, because they are washed too often and brushed too little. Here is what we tell everyone who takes one home.

Brush it

Wool cleans itself better than you would think. After a day out, give the coat a few firm strokes with a natural-bristle clothes brush, downwards, with the grain. It lifts dust and dirt off the surface before they work their way in. Two minutes a week does more good than any dry cleaner.

Hang it properly

Use a wide wooden hanger that fills the shoulders, never a wire one. Leave space around the coat in the wardrobe; wool that is crushed between other clothes cannot recover its shape. If it has been rained on, let it dry at room temperature, away from the radiator.

Air it, do not wash it

Most smells leave a wool coat after a night by an open window. Washing strips the natural oils that make wool resist water and dirt in the first place. If it really needs cleaning, take it to someone who cleans wool by hand, once a year at most.

Moth

Moths like wool that is dirty, dark and left alone. Brushing and airing are the best defence. Cedar blocks help a little; lavender helps mostly the wardrobe’s smell. If you find a hole, do not throw the coat away. Send it back to us and we will mend it, for the cost of the post, for as long as we are here.

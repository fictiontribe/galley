The label

We make coats, and the knits that go under them. Forty of each, cut by hand in Marrowby from cloth we can trace back to the sheep.

No sales, no drops, no seasons off. When a coat sells out, we make it again next year, a little better.

The workroom

Every coat is cut on a table you can see from the street.

Four of us, one long table, and the windows of a rope-maker's loft. The cloth arrives in rolls from two mills we have visited; the coats leave folded in paper, one at a time.

Come on a Thursday and you can watch one being made.

In the workroom

How a coat is made, in 3 days

  1. 01

    Day 1, the cloth

    The Shetland comes off the roll and rests on the table for a day, so it has stopped moving before anyone cuts it.

  2. 02

    Day 2, the cut

    Every piece is marked out in chalk along a wooden rule and cut by hand, forty at a time, from one paper pattern.

  3. 03

    Day 3, the coat

    Sewn, pressed and worn round the room for a week before it is brushed, folded in paper and sent to a stockist.
